The project

FireBridge, the harmonization layer.

An open-source Python pipeline that reads both NASA fire records, pairs every MODIS pixel with the VIIRS hotspots inside it, learns the difference with machine learning, and writes a harmonized daily fire grid plus this interactive site.

How it works

Three steps, validated on days the model never saw.

01

Match

A KD-tree on 3-D Earth coordinates finds every VIIRS hotspot inside each MODIS pixel footprint from the same overpass window: 84,212 matched pairs.

02

Learn

A gradient-boosting model learns when MODIS sees what VIIRS sees, from fire power, brightness, scan angle, latitude and day/night. A robust regression compares their fire power.

03

Translate

A Poisson model converts VIIRS cell statistics into MODIS-equivalent fire counts and fire power on a 0.25° daily grid. We trained on 6 days and tested on 2 held-out days.

What we discovered

Aqua's orbit has drifted.

We expected MODIS-Aqua and VIIRS to pass overhead at the same time. They don't anymore: Aqua now crosses a median of 143 minutes after NOAA-21. Methods that assume simultaneous passes break, so we measured the real offset and built it into the matching.

  • Only 25% of VIIRS hotspots are also seen by MODIS. Most of the gap comes from small fires under 5 MW.
  • At night MODIS catches 2–3× more small fires than by day, because there is no sunlight drowning out the heat signal.

FAQ

Questions, answered clearly.

Why do MODIS and VIIRS disagree so much?

VIIRS pixels are about 7× smaller in area (375 m vs 1 km), so a small fire fills a bigger share of the pixel and is easier to detect. One MODIS fire pixel typically contains ~4 VIIRS hotspots. The two satellites also no longer pass overhead at the same time.

Why does it matter if MODIS retires?

Fire trends, smoke and carbon-emission inventories are built on the MODIS record, which starts in 2000. If VIIRS simply replaces it, the series would show a huge artificial jump. A harmonized, MODIS-equivalent VIIRS record keeps the 25-year series comparable.

How do you know the model works?

We trained on the first 6 days and validated on the last 2 days, which the model never saw, against real MODIS-Aqua observations. The total count bias went from +695% to −13%, and the error per cell-day dropped about 6×.

What NASA data did you use?

NASA FIRMS near-real-time active-fire data: MODIS Collection 6.1 (Terra and Aqua, MCD14DL) and VIIRS 375 m Collection 2 from NOAA-21, global, 8–15 November 2026. Both are free and need no login. The satellite imagery in “Seen from space” is streamed from NASA GIBS: Landsat (WELD) annual composites, Harmonized Landsat Sentinel-2 (HLS) and MODIS Terra corrected reflectance.

What are the limitations?

This run uses one week of data. Training on the multi-year FIRMS archive (2012 onwards) with per-biome models would make it much stronger. Part of the disagreement is real fire change in the ~2.4 h between overpasses, which a diurnal-cycle model could separate.

Did you use AI tools?

Yes. An AI assistant (Claude) helped write and debug parts of the code, this website and the text. The team defined the approach, ran everything on real NASA data and reviewed the results. No AI-generated images are used; every chart is drawn from the data.

Who is QuasarAI?

QuasarAI is the team behind FireBridge. A quasar is the brightest object in the universe; we turn massive amounts of space data into clear, useful insight.
